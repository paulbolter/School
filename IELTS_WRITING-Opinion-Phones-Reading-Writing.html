IELTS Academic · Writing Task 2

The Opinion Essay

Do you agree or disagree? · 250 words minimum · ~40 minutes
Your Task
The use of mobile phones and computers for communication has affected our reading and writing skills.
Do you agree or disagree?
Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.

What kind of question is this?

TypeOpinion The question asks for your view. You must choose a position — agree or disagree — and defend it with reasons all the way through.

"Do you agree or disagree?" Decide your position before you start writing and state it clearly in your introduction. Keep the same opinion from the first line to the last — don't switch sides halfway through.
Tip: The word "affected" usually means a negative effect here — that phones and computers have weakened our reading and writing. If you agree, you think these skills have got worse. If you disagree, you think they have not been harmed — or have even improved.

How to structure it

  • Introduction: rephrase the topic and state your opinion clearly.
  • Body 1: give your first reason — explain it and add an example.
  • Body 2: give your second reason — explain it and add an example.
  • Conclusion: restate your opinion and sum up your two reasons.
One position, two reasons. Unlike the "outweigh" essay, both body paragraphs argue for the same side here. Pick your two strongest reasons and develop each one fully.
Introduction · State your opinion

Rephrase the topic

e.g. "Today most people communicate through phones and computers rather than by writing on paper or reading printed books."

Then state your opinion

Make it clear that you agree — these devices have weakened our reading and writing skills.

Body 1 · Your first reason — writing skills

Ideas you could use — pick one or two to develop

  • Chat apps encourage short forms and slang ("u", "gr8") that slip into formal writing
  • Autocorrect and predictive text mean people stop practising spelling
  • Emojis and short messages replace full, grammatical sentences
  • People rarely write longer pieces by hand any more
Body 2 · Your second reason — reading skills

Ideas you could use — pick one or two to develop

  • Constant notifications make it hard to concentrate on long texts
  • People skim short posts instead of reading books or articles carefully
  • Attention spans grow shorter, so deep reading becomes difficult
  • Fewer young people read for pleasure now
Conclusion · Restate your opinion

Restate your opinion clearly

Make it obvious you still agree.

Sum up your two reasons

You may use "Because" or "Since" to combine them into one complex sentence.

A strong final phrase

e.g. "For these reasons, I strongly agree that these devices have harmed our reading and writing."

Introduction · State your opinion

Rephrase the topic

e.g. "Today most people communicate through phones and computers rather than by writing on paper or reading printed books."

Then state your opinion

Make it clear that you disagree — these devices have not harmed (and may have improved) our reading and writing skills.

Body 1 · Your first reason — writing skills

Ideas you could use — pick one or two to develop

  • People actually write far more now — messages, posts and emails every day
  • Daily typing keeps writing skills active and in constant use
  • Grammar checkers and online tools help people spot and fix mistakes
  • Online communities let people share writing and get quick feedback
Body 2 · Your second reason — reading skills

Ideas you could use — pick one or two to develop

  • People read constantly on screens — news, articles, captions and messages
  • The internet gives access to far more reading material than ever before
  • Translation tools make reading in a second language much easier
  • Whether skills improve depends on the person and their education, not the device
Conclusion · Restate your opinion

Restate your opinion clearly

Make it obvious you still disagree.

Sum up your two reasons

You may use "Because" or "Since" to combine them into one complex sentence.

A strong final phrase

e.g. "For these reasons, I firmly disagree that these devices have harmed our reading and writing."
